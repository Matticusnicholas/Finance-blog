Tax-Loss Harvesting: Free Money You're Probably Leaving on the Table

November 20, 2025 · 10 min read

When the market drops, most people feel bad about their portfolio losses. I've learned to see downturns differently: as tax-saving opportunities. That's what tax-loss harvesting is all about – turning paper losses into real tax savings.

The Basic Concept

Tax-loss harvesting means selling investments that have dropped in value to "realize" the loss. You can then use that loss to offset gains elsewhere, reducing your tax bill.

Simple example: You have $5,000 in gains from selling Stock A. You also have Stock B, which you bought for $10,000 but is now worth $6,000 (a $4,000 loss). If you sell Stock B, you can use the $4,000 loss to offset your $5,000 gain. Now you only pay taxes on $1,000 in gains instead of $5,000.

If you're in the 15% long-term capital gains bracket, that's $600 in tax savings.

It Gets Better: $3,000 Against Ordinary Income

If your losses exceed your gains (or you have no gains), you can deduct up to $3,000 in capital losses against your ordinary income each year.

Ordinary income is taxed at higher rates than capital gains. If you're in the 22% bracket, that $3,000 deduction saves you $660 in taxes.

Losses beyond $3,000 carry forward to future years indefinitely. So if you have $10,000 in losses and no gains, you can use $3,000 this year and carry forward $7,000 for future years.

The Key: Stay Invested

Here's what makes this powerful: you don't have to stay out of the market. You sell your losing investment, realize the loss for tax purposes, and immediately buy something similar (but not identical – more on this in a moment).

Your portfolio stays essentially the same. Your market exposure is unchanged. But you've locked in a tax benefit.

The Wash Sale Rule

There's one important rule: the wash sale rule prevents you from selling a security at a loss and buying it back (or something "substantially identical") within 30 days before or after the sale.

If you violate this rule, the loss is disallowed for tax purposes.

Here's how to work around it legally:

Option 1: Swap for similar (but not identical) fund

Sell VTI (Vanguard Total Stock Market) at a loss. Buy ITOT (iShares Total Stock Market) immediately. Both track the total US market, so your exposure is the same, but they're different securities so no wash sale.

After 31 days, you can swap back to VTI if you prefer it.

Option 2: Wait 31 days

If you sell at a loss and want the exact same security back, just wait 31 days. During that time, you could be in cash (risky if market rises) or in a similar fund.

Practical Example

Let's say in a down market, you have:

  • VTI shares bought at $200, now worth $170. Loss per share: $30.
  • You own 100 shares. Total loss: $3,000.

You sell all 100 shares, realizing a $3,000 loss. Immediately (same day is fine), you buy $17,000 of ITOT (similar fund).

Your market exposure: unchanged. You still own ~$17,000 of US total market.

Your tax benefit: $3,000 loss that can offset gains or income.

Tax savings: $660 (at 22% bracket) to $1,110 (at 37% bracket).

You've manufactured real tax savings while staying fully invested. This is free money (well, relatively – there may be small trading costs).

When to Harvest Losses

Market downturns

The best time is during significant market drops. 2022 was a great year for harvesting – the market was down significantly, creating lots of loss opportunities.

Year-end

Review your portfolio in December. If you have gains for the year, look for losses to offset them.

Throughout the year (automated)

Services like Wealthfront and Betterment do tax-loss harvesting automatically. They monitor your portfolio and harvest losses whenever opportunities arise.

What Not to Do

Don't harvest in tax-advantaged accounts

Losses in 401(k)s, IRAs, or Roth accounts don't matter for taxes. Only harvest in taxable brokerage accounts.

Don't forget the wash sale rule

If you're harvesting VTI in your brokerage account, make sure your 401(k) isn't set to auto-buy VTI in the next 30 days. That would trigger a wash sale.

Don't harvest for the sake of harvesting

If you don't have gains to offset and your income is low, the benefit might not be worth the hassle. The $3,000 annual deduction is nice but not life-changing.

Don't let the tax tail wag the investment dog

Don't make bad investment decisions just for tax benefits. The primary goal is still good investing; tax-loss harvesting is optimization, not strategy.

Long-Term vs. Short-Term Considerations

Capital gains and losses are categorized as long-term (held over 1 year) or short-term (held 1 year or less).

Short-term losses first offset short-term gains (taxed at ordinary income rates – up to 37%). Then long-term losses offset long-term gains (taxed at 0-20%).

Ideally, you want to harvest short-term losses because they offset income taxed at higher rates. But any loss is helpful.

The Cost Basis Reset

One thing to understand: when you harvest a loss and buy a similar fund, your new cost basis is lower. This means more taxable gains when you eventually sell.

Example:

  • Buy VTI at $200
  • Sell at $170 (harvest $30 loss)
  • Buy ITOT at $170
  • Later sell ITOT at $250
  • Gain: $80 (from $170 basis), not $50 (from original $200 basis)

You've deferred the tax, not eliminated it. But deferral has value – you get to invest that tax savings now, potentially for decades.

Also, if you hold until death, your heirs get a "step-up" in cost basis, potentially eliminating the deferred gain entirely.

Is It Worth the Effort?

For most people, yes, especially if:

  • You have taxable investment accounts (not just 401(k)/IRA)
  • You have significant capital gains to offset
  • You're in a higher tax bracket
  • The market has dropped, creating loss opportunities

If you only have retirement accounts, tax-loss harvesting isn't relevant to you.

Doing It Yourself vs. Automation

You can tax-loss harvest manually – just review your portfolio periodically, identify losses, execute the trades, and track everything for your tax return.

Or you can use a robo-advisor like Wealthfront or Betterment that does it automatically. They claim to add 0.5-1.5% annually in tax savings for typical clients. For larger portfolios, this can be significant.

DIY is fine for simple portfolios. Automation is nice for complex ones or if you don't want to think about it.

My Approach

I manually check for harvesting opportunities twice per year: once mid-year after any significant market drop, and once in December before year-end. It takes about 30 minutes each time. For my portfolio size, this is worth hundreds of dollars in annual tax savings.

Tax-loss harvesting isn't sexy, but it's free money. In a down market, it's one of the few silver linings – turning your paper losses into real tax savings while staying fully invested.
