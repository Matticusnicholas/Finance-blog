Coast FIRE: The More Achievable Path to Financial Freedom

December 8, 2025 · 12 min read

Traditional FIRE feels impossible? You're not alone. The idea of saving 50-70% of your income for a decade feels unrealistic for most people. But there's a middle ground that might work better: Coast FIRE.

Coast FIRE changed how I think about financial independence, and it might be the version of FIRE that actually fits your life.

What Is Coast FIRE?

Coast FIRE means you've saved enough that, even if you never invest another dollar, your existing investments will grow to support a traditional retirement (usually age 65) through compound interest alone.

Once you hit your Coast FIRE number, you can "coast" – you only need to earn enough to cover your current expenses. No more retirement savings required. The math is already done.

This is different from traditional FIRE, where you save enough to retire RIGHT NOW. Coast FIRE says: "I've got retirement handled. Now I just need to cover today's bills."

The Math Behind Coast FIRE

Let's work through an example:

Say you want $1,500,000 at age 65 to support a $60,000/year retirement (using the 4% rule).

If you're 25 years old, you have 40 years for your money to grow. At 7% average returns, money doubles roughly every 10 years.

Working backwards:

  • $1,500,000 at 65
  • $750,000 at 55 (one doubling period before)
  • $375,000 at 45 (two periods before)
  • $187,500 at 35 (three periods before)
  • $93,750 at 25 (four periods before)

If you're 25 and have ~$94,000 invested, you could theoretically stop saving for retirement entirely and still have $1.5 million at 65.

If you're 35 and have ~$188,000 invested, same deal.

This is your Coast FIRE number – the amount where compound interest takes over.

Coast FIRE Numbers by Age

Here are approximate Coast FIRE numbers for different ages, assuming you want $1,500,000 at age 65 (7% real returns):

Current Age Years Until 65 Coast FIRE Number
25 40 ~$100,000
30 35 ~$140,000
35 30 ~$197,000
40 25 ~$276,000
45 20 ~$388,000

If you want more at 65 (say, $2,000,000), multiply these numbers by 1.33. Want less ($1,000,000)? Multiply by 0.67.

Why Coast FIRE Is More Achievable

The target is much lower

Traditional FIRE for $60,000/year expenses requires $1,500,000. Coast FIRE for a 30-year-old requires ~$140,000. That's less than 10% of the full FIRE number.

You only need to save intensely for a few years

If you can save aggressively in your 20s and early 30s, you can hit your Coast FIRE number and then relax. You don't need to maintain high savings rates forever.

You still have income flexibility

After hitting Coast FIRE, you still work – but you can take lower-paying work you enjoy, work part-time, take career risks, or take sabbaticals. You just need to cover current expenses.

It reduces pressure

Traditional FIRE requires sustained extreme savings. Coast FIRE front-loads the effort. Many people find this more psychologically sustainable.

My Coast FIRE Strategy

I'm 35 and have about $250,000 invested. Based on the math above, I've technically already hit Coast FIRE for a $1.5M retirement at 65.

But I'm not stopping contributions entirely. Here's what I'm doing instead:

  1. Reduced contribution intensity. Instead of maxing every account, I contribute enough for employer match plus a bit more. Maybe 15% of income instead of 40%.
  2. More flexibility in career choices. Knowing retirement is "handled" lets me take more risks – pursuing work I find meaningful even if it pays less.
  3. More present enjoyment. I'm spending more on experiences and things I value now, since the retirement pressure is off.
  4. Targeting earlier than 65. By continuing to contribute at a moderate rate, I'm actually on track for a traditional FIRE at 50-55, not just Coast FIRE at 65.

The Coast FIRE Lifestyle

What does life look like after hitting Coast FIRE?

Career options open up

You could:

  • Switch to a lower-stress, lower-paying job
  • Work part-time
  • Start a business without worrying about retirement savings
  • Take time off between jobs
  • Pursue creative or passion projects

You still need income

Coast FIRE isn't retirement. You need to cover rent, food, healthcare, and all current expenses. The only thing you DON'T need to save for is retirement.

Your burn rate matters more

If your expenses are $4,000/month, you need to earn ~$4,000/month (after taxes). If you can cut expenses to $2,500/month, suddenly part-time work or a passion job becomes viable.

Coast FIRE Calculator

To figure out your Coast FIRE number:

  1. Decide your target retirement age (65 is standard)
  2. Estimate your annual expenses in retirement (in today's dollars)
  3. Multiply by 25 (that's your target portfolio at retirement)
  4. Use a compound interest calculator to figure out how much you need today to reach that number

Or use this formula:

Coast FIRE Number = Target Portfolio / (1.07 ^ Years Until Retirement)

Example: $1,500,000 / (1.07^30) = $197,000 at age 35 for retirement at 65.

Critiques and Considerations

7% returns aren't guaranteed

The math assumes 7% real returns. If the market underperforms, you might not hit your target. Consider using a more conservative assumption (5-6%) or continuing to contribute modestly even after "coasting."

Inflation risk

If inflation is higher than expected, your target might not be enough. Building in some cushion is wise.

You might want more

Coast FIRE assumes modest retirement spending. If your lifestyle expectations change, you might wish you'd saved more.

Healthcare (US)

If you're reducing work to part-time, you might lose employer healthcare. Factor in health insurance costs before making drastic changes.

Coast FIRE vs. Other FIRE Variants

Type Description When You Can Stop Working
Traditional FIRE Full financial independence Immediately
Coast FIRE Retirement savings done; cover current expenses At traditional retirement age
Barista FIRE Part-time work covers expenses + benefits When investments + part-time work cover needs
Lean FIRE FIRE on minimal expenses Immediately, on tight budget

Who Coast FIRE Works Best For

  • People who enjoy working but want less pressure
  • People who started saving early and have time for compounding
  • People who want balance between present enjoyment and future security
  • People who find traditional FIRE too extreme or stressful
  • People in expensive cities where traditional FIRE numbers feel impossible

The Bottom Line

Coast FIRE offers a middle path between the traditional retirement timeline (work until 65, then hope you saved enough) and extreme FIRE (save 70% of your income for a decade).

It's based on a simple insight: compound interest is powerful, and front-loading your retirement savings gives you decades of growth. Once you've got enough invested, time does the work for you.

For many people, Coast FIRE is the realistic version of financial independence – achievable, sustainable, and life-improving even if you never fully "retire early."
